# Green Solutions Manufacturing Ltd. — Credit & Risk Assessment

**A lender's-eye view of a fast-growing manufacturer: is Green Solutions' 2023 growth sustainable, or is it outrunning its balance sheet?**

## The Business Question

Green Solutions Manufacturing Ltd. grew revenue by 70.7% in FY2023 while simultaneously cutting its leverage from 7.82x to 1.24x — an unusual combination. Before extending or renewing credit, a lender needs to know: **is this genuine, well-financed growth, or is the company's cash position quietly deteriorating underneath a strong income statement?**

## Headline Findings

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | £31.54M | £53.82M | +70.7% YoY — exceptional top-line growth |
| Net Profit After Tax | £0.86M | £5.64M | Profit scaled even faster than revenue |
| EBITDA Margin | 4.74% | 10.26% | Operating scaling is working, not just top-line growth |
| Leverage Metric | 7.82x | 1.24x | Aggressive, genuine de-leveraging |
| Interest Cover | — | 17.53x | Very strong — debt servicing is not a near-term concern |
| Operating Cash Flow | — | £11.45M | Nearly doubled year-on-year |
| Free Cash Flow | — | **–£8.00M** | Negative — the company is reinvesting faster than it's generating cash |
| Current Ratio | 1.88x | 1.38x | Declined but still above the 1.0x solvency benchmark |

## Credit Assessment

**The underlying credit profile is strong**, not fragile: profitability, margin expansion, and de-leveraging are all moving in the right direction simultaneously, and interest coverage (17.53x) leaves a wide safety margin on existing debt obligations.

**The one flag worth watching is free cash flow.** Negative FCF (–£8.00M) despite strong operating cash flow is explained by heavy CAPEX (£8.01M) — this is a company in an aggressive investment/growth phase, not one struggling to generate cash from operations. That distinction matters for a lending decision: it's a financing-timing question, not a solvency question.

**Recommendation:** creditworthy for continued or expanded facilities, with covenants tied to FCF turning positive within a defined horizon (e.g. 18–24 months) as CAPEX intensity normalizes — rather than tightening credit now based on the FCF number in isolation.
